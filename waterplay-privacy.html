Water Play — Privacy Policy

Last updated 10 September 2026

Water Play does not collect any data about you or your child. None at all.

It is a kitchen sink to play with, made for young children and for autistic children who are drawn to water, so the shortest honest privacy policy is the right one. This page explains what that means precisely.

What is collected

Nothing. The app has no accounts, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party software development kits of any kind. It does not ask for your name, your email address, your location, your contacts, your photos, or your microphone or camera.

What leaves the device

Nothing. Water Play contains no networking code whatsoever. It makes no requests to any server, including the developer's own, and it works exactly the same with the device in aeroplane mode. There are no in-app purchases and no subscriptions, so there is not even a payment transaction to describe.

What is stored, and where

Nothing. Unlike most apps, Water Play does not save anything at all between launches — no settings, no progress, no counters, no files. Every time it opens, the sink starts in the same state: half full, plug in, cold tap running. There is no stored data to upload, to leak, or to ask to have deleted.

Children

Water Play is designed for children and complies with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and Apple's Kids Category requirements. Because the app collects no personal information whatsoever, there is nothing to disclose, nothing to share, and nothing for a parent to request or delete beyond removing the app.

The app contains no advertising, no in-app purchases, no links to websites, no social media features, and no way to communicate with anyone. There is nothing in it that can take a child out of the app or put them in contact with another person.

Tracking

Water Play does not track users, does not use the Advertising Identifier (IDFA), and does not share any data with data brokers or advertising networks. It will never present the App Tracking Transparency prompt, because there is nothing it would ask permission for.

What the app does use

Three things, all of them local to the device and none of them recorded: the touchscreen, so the water can be splashed; the speaker, to play sounds that are generated by the app itself rather than streamed from anywhere; and the vibration motor, for the small taps you feel when something is pressed. The app never turns on the microphone or the camera, and has no code capable of doing so.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever begins collecting anything, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the change will be described here rather than quietly absorbed.
